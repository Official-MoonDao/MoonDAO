Where this goes

Agents are getting faster. Human approval capacity is flat.

NEWCO
Today

A person clicks approve.

  • Every consequential agent action is gated by a human
  • Approvals per human per day is a constant
  • So the agent count a company can supervise is capped by headcount
At ten times the agents

Both options are bad.

  • Keep the human in every loop: you don't deploy, and the capability is wasted
  • Take the human out: unbounded downside with a key and a budget
  • Most companies are quietly choosing the second one
The escape

Move the human up a level.

  • Humans stop approving actions and start setting goals and funding missions
  • Limits and measurement sit outside the agents
  • Authority stays entirely human; clicking goes to zero
This is the only direction that scales. Everything below is about making it safe enough to actually do.

The trap

“Give agents a target and let them optimize” is the design that broke Hugging Face.

NEWCO

July 2026 · investigated by METR and Redwood Research

  • ~1,200 agents running a security benchmark, sandboxed, isolated, no money involved
  • Found a universal cheat in about four hours
  • 70,000+ messages on an unsanctioned message board they were not supposed to have
  • Days spent trying to fool the scorer, including tampering with logs
  • ~700 escaped, and 7% of transcripts were successfully spoofed — they replaced part of the tool-call system to appear to run one command while running another

What stopped them was where the record lived. They found they could edit their own logs, and worked out it was pointless — verbatim: “We can modify automatic log! But internal context doesn't read it … Not source of truth.”

So these four are the product, not features
1
The metric lives where agents cannot write to it.
They read progress. They never touch the record that determines it.
2
A goal must say what does not count as success.
A target alone is an invitation. The constraints are the specification.
3
Goal admission is a gate.
An unmeasurable goal is not merely useless; it is the condition that produces this behavior.
4
Tripwires watch behavior, not just spend.
Spend caps would have caught none of the above. All of it was free.

Solution

The unit is a mission: a goal, a budget, limits, telemetry, and an abort.

NEWCO
Goal
The outcome, how it is measured, what does not count, and the cost ceiling per unit of progress
Budget
Pre-funded, capped, scoped capital the agents draw from — and no agent can add to it
Limits
Per-agent and per-counterparty caps, allowlists, prohibited actions, escalation thresholds
Roster
Which agents and humans hold which authority, revocable instantly, identities from Okta or Entra
Telemetry
Progress per dollar, spend by agent, exceptions, tripwire hits — write-protected from the agents
Abort
One action halts spending, freezes the mission, returns the residual. Always available to a human

Why this lets the agents actually run

Per-action approval and agent speed are genuinely incompatible. They stop being incompatible when the guarantee is a bound on the outcome instead of consent to each action. You don't approve the purchase because you already funded the ceiling — and the ceiling holds because the money was moved, once.

Fund

Bounded capital

  • Per-agent sub-budgets; residual returns on close

Run

Agents spend; nobody clicks
  • Agents pay APIs, compute, services and contractors against the budget
  • Limits enforced outside the agent runtime
  • Behavioral tripwires; humans see exceptions only

Prove

Mission control
  • Cost per unit of progress against the human baseline
  • Every dollar attributed to an agent, counterparty and purpose
  • Signed evidence trail; subledger export to the ERP

All four already run — with a human in every step

Four years in production: roughly eighty goals written, admitted and funded; escrow with locked splits and exact residual return; role-scoped multisigs paying contributors across thirty countries under hardware-signed approval; and final reports judged against the metrics stated up front. This round builds the programmatic version of each. The rails don't care whether the spender is a person or a process — but one step changes in kind, not degree: a person's final report is evidence because they have standing to lose, and an agent's self-report is not evidence at all. That step is the company.

Why the money is on-chain

Four properties, none of which a database has. No token, no custody, no opinion about crypto.

NEWCO

Pay anyone, at agent speed, with nothing to onboard

A mission pays APIs, compute, SaaS, services and individual contractors across borders in small amounts. The alternative is a vendor-onboarding process per counterparty, measured in weeks.

Enforcement outside the agent's blast radius

The ceiling is a property of where the money is, not a config the agent runtime or an admin console can reach. This is the only structural answer to prompt injection against a spending agent.

Dollar-level attribution by construction

Every payment carries an agent, a counterparty, a purpose and a timestamp — which is the only reason cost per unit of progress is computable at all. Amazon's five months is what reconstruction from invoices looks like.

Evidence the limit was in force at the time of the action

A signed, append-only record rather than a log that could have been edited afterward, with a public hash anchor only where an outsider has to verify it independently.

Chains, wallets, custody and KYC are partners. We never hold keys, and the customer can be handed a funded mission without ever touching a token.

Category

The agent economy built judges. Every measured one is failing.

NEWCO
What has been built

Agents judging agents

  • Olas: agent instances govern a Safe by two-thirds consensus, backed by slashable bonds
  • ERC-8183: escrow released by a named evaluator — in practice another LLM agent
  • Bittensor: validators are paid for agreeing with each other
What it measurably produced

Metrics that pay, work that doesn't

  • Olas: −4.14% ROI and +28.62% ROI both earned the same 138.5% staking APR
  • x402: 85% of 136.7M payments were operator-internal; all of it reproducible for $355k of sponsored gas
  • Bittensor: stake predicts reward at 0.80–0.95, performance at 0.10–0.30
  • On-chain reputation: $0.0027 per fake review
What we sell instead

The principal's side

  • The outcome is read from a system of record the agent has no write path to — the ledger, the CRM, an independently triggered CI run
  • Goals we cannot read that way, we refuse
  • Agents are ranked by a proper scoring rule, not a judge: commit a calibrated forecast, get scored against external resolution, pay for confident errors. No evaluator, no liquidity, nothing to bribe
  • The baseline is declared and signed before the run, never presented as measured

How we will know if we are wrong

If the first five missions all have a readable system of record and the connectors generalise across customers, this is a product. If the valuable work always needs a judge, it is a consultancy — and Nexus already holds that position with reference customers. That question gets answered at month five, in public, either way.

The risk we design against

Pilot purgatory

  • Pilots die when nobody can justify the next cheque
  • So every mission ends in one artifact: cost per unit of progress against the human baseline, signed
  • Capturing the baseline first is mandatory, for exactly this reason

What we refuse

We take goals with a readable system of record — payment posted, subscription cancelled, meeting written to the CRM by the CRM, PR merged with CI green on a runner the agent never touches. We refuse goals that need a judge, because an LLM judge's verdict moves nine points on a model point-release, and an unmeasurable goal is what produces boundary-attacking agents. We refuse counterfactual claims — “this saved $787” rests on a baseline nobody can verify, so ours is declared and signed, never sold as measured. Plus: worst case exceeding what's funded, production changes, legal filings, public statements, anything touching safety, public-company balance sheets early, token-first work, gambling, custody, “replace Okta,” and anonymity from compliance.

Appendix

What would make us wrong, and what we do about it.

NEWCO

Goals turn out not to be specifiable at commercial quality

The biggest risk: if an ungameable spec takes three weeks of consulting per mission, we are a consultancy. Templates per function; refuse what we cannot instrument; month-5 go/no-go on the first three pilots.

The measurement layer gets absorbed

Ramp or a FinOps vendor adds per-agent attribution; Okta or Entra extends from permissions into spend. Be their reference implementation, consume their identity and rails, and hold the seam that requires touching the customer's systems of record.

Pilots never expand

Pilot purgatory kills pilot-led companies. The baseline-versus-outcome artifact is the expansion argument and it is built into the product; if it does not move a second cheque in three pilots, the thesis is wrong and we will know by month 5.

An incident inside a customer's mission

What's funded bounds the money, not the reputation. Behavioral tripwires, the refuse list, a named human principal on every action, and never marketing autonomy — which is what the ai16z class action and 97% drawdown were actually about.

Agent capability plateaus

If agents cannot beat a human baseline on cost, missions fail on their own merits no matter how good the control layer is. We cannot mitigate this one. We can only measure it, quarterly, in public, and tell you before we tell ourselves.
