# Compensation recommendation — Social Media Manager (X + Instagram)

**Internal.** Not for the public posting. The posting states only the range and that it is
confirmed in the next Executive Branch budgeting cycle.

---

## Recommendation

**$2,000–$2,800 per month** for ≤10 hours/week, fiat and/or $MOONEY at the contributor's
election, entered through a paid one-month trial at roughly $750–$1,000.

That is about **$45–$65/hour**, or **$24k–$34k/year** for the part-time commitment.

Reserve anything above $3,000/month for a candidate who is demonstrably exceptional, and
structure that top end as base plus performance rather than a higher fixed retainer.

## Correcting the earlier $3,000–$4,500 figure

An earlier draft of this document recommended $3,000–$4,500/month. That was too high, for two
compounding reasons:

1. **Premiums stacked on premiums.** The inputs were freelance bands that already reflect
   specialist pricing, and then a "dual niche premium" of 30–50% was applied on top, and then a
   "proven results and existing following" premium on top of that. Each step was individually
   arguable; multiplied together they drifted far from what this scope actually clears for.
2. **The sanity check was read backwards.** $3,000–$4,500/month at 10 hrs/week implies a
   full-time equivalent of $140k–$200k. That was presented as confirmation. It is the opposite:
   $200k FTE is senior in-house marketing leadership, and this is one operator producing content
   for two channels ten hours a week. An implied FTE that lands well above what the org pays its
   engineers is the signal to re-derive the number, not to publish it.

## What ten hours a week actually buys

Sizing the work makes the rate obvious. Ten hours is roughly:

| Activity | Hours/week |
|---|---|
| Planning and content calendar | ~1 |
| Content production (threads, carousels, short-form video) | ~4–5 |
| Engagement, replies, monitoring the relevant conversations | ~3 |
| Measurement and reporting | ~1 |

That is a thin week. It is an operator with good taste and good context executing a plan — not a
strategist rebuilding a brand, and not someone managing a content team or a paid-ads budget. The
$4,000–$8,000/month retainers in the market research cover that fuller scope: multiple channels,
paid media, a production team, and reporting into a marketing leader.

## How this is actually contracted

For a project with a 30–50k follower account it is trying to grow, the common arrangements:

| Structure | Typical shape | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat monthly retainer** | $1,500–$2,500/mo for one primary channel plus a light secondary, with defined deliverables | The default, and what fits here |
| **Retainer + performance bonus** | $1,500–$2,000 base plus up to ~$1,000 against agreed milestones | Outcomes matter and fit is unproven — a good fit here |
| **Paid trial, then retainer** | One month at ~$750–$1,000 for reduced scope | Near-universal first step |
| **Per-deliverable** | ~$150–$400 per thread, ~$75–$150 per carousel | Spiky or very low volume |
| **Hourly** | $30–$60/hr | Ad-hoc work that is hard to scope |
| **Community stipend** | $500–$1,500/mo, often mostly in tokens | Someone already active in the project |

Two notes on the outer edges. Hourly rates of $75–$150 are real, but they are *consulting* rates
— an audit, a strategy document, training your team — not execution; nobody pays $100/hour for
someone to schedule posts, and hourly also invites timesheet arguments about a role we have said
we will judge on outcomes. And X ghostwriting retainers of $3k–$5k+ are real too, but they are
overwhelmingly for founders and operators with a direct revenue line, where the writer's output
is attributable to sales.

## The requirement that actually drives the price

The single biggest cost driver in the posting is **"an existing personal following and/or proven
ability to reach the right audiences."** It is also the requirement with the weakest link to the
day-to-day work.

Reach and execution are two different purchases, and bundling them is what pushes the rate toward
$4,000. Unbundled:

- **Execution** — running the accounts well — clears at $2,000–$2,500/month for this scope.
- **Reach** — someone else's audience amplifying us — is bought per campaign, as an influencer or
  amplification deal, when there is something specific worth amplifying.

If we keep the existing-following requirement as a hard filter, we are paying a premium for
distribution we may not use, and $2,800–$3,200 becomes the honest number. If we relax it to a
nice-to-have, the pool widens considerably at $2,000–$2,500 and we can spend the difference on
targeted amplification when a campaign warrants it. **My recommendation is to relax it**, and to
keep "documented growth you personally drove" as the hard filter, since that is the requirement
that actually predicts performance in the role.

## Two MoonDAO-specific factors

**Internal equity is public here.** Every payment is auditable, and contributors can compare. A
part-time social contractor at ~$104/hour — where $4,500/month lands — sitting next to engineers
and ops contributors at lower effective rates is a governance problem waiting to surface in a
Town Hall, and it is a fair question for a $MOONEY holder to ask. $45–$65/hour does not raise
that question.

**The best candidate may already be in the community.** The dual space-plus-crypto literacy
requirement is what makes this role look expensive on paper, because importing it is genuinely
hard. But MoonDAO has ~38k followers, an active Discord, Citizens, and weekly Town Halls. Someone
already in that orbit arrives with the context that would otherwise command the premium. Post
this internally — Discord, Town Hall, the newsletter — before concluding that the open market
rate is what we have to pay. The recruiting channel is a bigger lever on cost than the rate is.

## Structure

**Publish the range.** Unchanged from the earlier recommendation, and it matters more at a
sensible number than at an inflated one. Specialists screen out unpriced postings, an undisclosed
range sits badly next to a fully public treasury, and a stated range does most of the negotiation
before the first call.

**Lead with a paid trial.** One month at roughly $750–$1,000 for a defined slice of real work: a
thread, a carousel, and an engagement plan. It is the highest-signal step available for a role
whose central requirement is proven output, it de-risks a multi-month commitment for about a
third of one month's retainer, and it means we never ask for spec work.

**Use a performance component instead of a higher base.** A structure like $1,800/month base plus
up to $1,000/month against milestones agreed in week one gets a strong candidate to $2,800
without committing the treasury to that rate before anything has been demonstrated. It is also
squarely on-brand for an org that says it judges results rather than activity.

**Monthly retainer, not hourly.** Hourly is fine for the trial. Ongoing, a fixed retainer against
clear deliverables and a monthly report avoids scope creep in both directions.

**Fiat/$MOONEY split, contributor's choice.** A lower cash component is common in DAO roles when
the token has credible upside and the contributor is mission-aligned. Use $MOONEY to let an
aligned contributor take upside — not to discount the engagement below market.

**Review at 90 days**, then quarterly, against the targets set in week one. Say up front that
strong results lead to a rate increase or expanded scope; a specialist reads a static rate as a
ceiling.

## Budget-cycle sequencing

The interview window is 6 weeks and the salary lands in the next Executive Branch budgeting cycle.

1. **Budget the top of whichever band we choose, not the midpoint.** At $2,800/month with the
   option to hire below it, an unused headroom line costs nothing, while a request filed at
   $2,000 that meets a $2,800 candidate costs us the candidate or a full cycle. If we keep the
   existing-following requirement, file at $3,200.
2. **Fund the trial from the current cycle.** Roughly $1,000 should not wait on the next
   budgeting cycle. If it does, the 6-week window becomes 10 and the strongest candidates take
   other offers in the meantime.

## Reference points

- Freelance social media management, one primary channel plus a light secondary, part-time:
  $1,000–$2,500/month is the common retainer band.
- Freelance intermediate (2–5 yrs, multi-platform, content plus engagement): $40–$75/hr, or
  $1,500–$4,000/month at fuller scope than this role.
- Full-time remote US social media manager: ~$70k–$90k base mid-level. Straight pro-rata at 25%
  of a full-time load is ~$1,500–$1,900/month; our range sits above that, which is appropriate
  for a contractor carrying niche context and no benefits.
- Web3 and crypto social roles carry a genuine premium over generic social work; freelance web3
  social engagements frequently sit at $1,000–$3,000/month.
- Space and aerospace social roles for technical audiences: ~$90k–$115k full-time, which pro-rates
  to roughly $1,900–$2,400/month at this commitment.

The earlier $75–$150/hr and $4,000–$8,000/month figures in the source research are accurate for
what they describe — senior consultants and full-scope multi-channel engagements. They are simply
not describing this role.
