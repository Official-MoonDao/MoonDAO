The case for a public space program

Getting to the Moon is an organizational problem.

MoonDAO is a space program run by its members. Eight million dollars raised in public, two people flown, and a constitution resting on the lunar surface — all of it decided by vote and recorded where anyone can check the work.

Every serious attempt to leave this planet has been organized the same way: a government sets a goal, a contractor executes it, and the public watches. It worked once. It has not worked often since.

MoonDAO was founded on a different assumption — that the hard part is no longer propulsion, but coordination. If thousands of people can pool capital, argue in the open, and settle the outcome in a way nobody can quietly reverse, then the ambition stops being a matter of permission.

In its first month the organization raised eight million dollars from more than twelve thousand people. It has since allocated over six hundred thousand dollars across seventy-five research and engineering projects, flown two ordinary people to space, and placed a written constitution on the surface of the Moon.

None of that was decided in a boardroom. Each was proposed by a member, debated in public, and put to a vote — and every dollar behind it can be traced by anyone who cares to look.

“The technology is not the bottleneck. Deciding together, in public, and then actually paying for it — that is the bottleneck.” From the MoonDAO constitution, delivered to the lunar surface, 2025

The programs

Six active · All member-funded
I

Human spaceflight

The first crowdraised astronaut was chosen by onchain vote; a second everyday person was chosen by open sweepstakes. Both have flown.

II

Research funding

More than $600,000 allocated to seventy-five projects, each approved by a vote of the membership rather than a committee.

III

Training

Zero-gravity flights and analog missions that prepare the next generation of travelers before there is anywhere to go.

IV

The network

The Space Acceleration Network connects individuals and organizations with funding, tools, and the operational support to build.

V

The constitution

A charter for self-governance, written by members and delivered to the lunar surface in early 2025.

VI

The treasury

Open by default. Every proposal, vote, and payment is published and independently verifiable.
