Section I

Purpose

Every serious attempt to leave this planet has been organized the same way: a government sets the goal, a contractor executes, and the public watches. That arrangement worked once. It has not worked often since.

MoonDAO proceeds from a different premise — that the binding constraint is no longer propulsion but coordination. If enough people can pool capital, argue in the open, and settle the outcome in a record nobody can quietly revise, the ambition stops being a question of permission.2

In its first month the organization raised eight million dollars from more than twelve thousand people. It has since allocated over six hundred thousand dollars across seventy-five research and engineering projects.

None of it was decided in a boardroom. Each item was proposed by a member, debated publicly, and put to a vote — and every dollar behind it remains traceable.3

Section V

Governance

Authority within MoonDAO is granted by commitment rather than title. Members lock $MOONEY to become voters and co-govern the treasury; voting weight scales with the duration of that commitment.

There is no application, no membership committee, and no discretionary spending authority. A proposal that passes is executed; a proposal that fails is archived alongside its vote record.

Two crewed selections have been made under this system. Coby Cotton was selected by onchain vote in 2022. Dr. Eiman Jahangir was selected by open sweepstakes in 2024.

Both selections, and the funds behind them, remain auditable line by line — which is the entire point of running a space program this way.
