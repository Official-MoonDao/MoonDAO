I developed KITE-1: Orbital Computing and Bitcoin Infrastructure Concept, a research and architectural proposal exploring how modular data-center infrastructure could be deployed and progressively scaled in orbit. The contribution focuses on a real emerging challenge: the rapid growth of AI, high-performance computing, and Bitcoin infrastructure is increasing demand for energy, cooling, land, and grid capacity on Earth. The concept integrates five main systems into a single architectural strategy: * a lightweight photovoltaic membrane oriented toward the Sun, * a compact modular compute core, * thermal separation between the computing and heat-rejection systems, * a radiator field oriented toward deep space, * and a phased deployment strategy using high-altitude balloon testing before orbital implementation. A major part of the work was defining a realistic growth path rather than proposing a single 1 MW spacecraft from the beginning. I also developed a phased deployment model beginning with near-space balloon testing at approximately 30–40 km altitude, followed by capsule release, an orbital kick stage, a small compute seed in Low Earth Orbit, and finally deployment of the full KITE architecture. The research addresses several core technical challenges, including: * orbital heat rejection, * radiation exposure, * launch mass, * photovoltaic area, * modular replacement of computing hardware, * in-space assembly, * and the economics of scaling from a small demonstrator to a megawatt-class system. The initial application explored is Bitcoin mining and orbital computing, but the architecture is intentionally broader and could support AI inference, Earth-observation processing, scientific computing, autonomous spacecraft, lunar missions, and future space settlements.
