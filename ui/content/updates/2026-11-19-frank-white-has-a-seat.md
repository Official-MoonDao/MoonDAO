## The name, and the man who stayed on the ground

The phrase showed up on a cross-country flight. White was working in Princeton with Gerard O'Neill's Space Studies Institute, thinking about what it would be like to live in one of O'Neill's proposed settlements and see the Earth in the sky every day. Looking out of the airplane, he thought the people who lived that way would have an overview of the planet: one place, no borders that mattered, everything connected. He used the term in public in 1985, on a poster at a Space Studies Institute meeting. The book came two years later.

> The Overview Effect is the experience of seeing the Earth from space and realizing the inherent unity and oneness of everything on the planet.

That is his sentence, from his own account of the 1987 book. Houghton Mifflin published *The Overview Effect: Space Exploration and Human Evolution* that year. A marketing department tried to change the title, because nobody would know what it meant. They read the book and changed it back. The fourth edition is the one in the campaign film, from Multiverse Publishing in 2021.

He did not invent the feeling and then go looking for quotes. He went to Houston to test whether the feeling existed. NASA told him he could not interview every astronaut, and offered him two if he would come. They also suggested retired astronauts, which he had not thought of. By the time the first edition went to press he had interviewed 16 people who had been to space. He later said the trip confirmed that seeing the Earth from a distance did something distinct, and that he was surprised how uncommon the full shift was. The hypothesis changed. What he kept, in his own account of the book, was a shared theme under the different tellings: the borders did not hold, and the planet looked like something that had to be protected. The 1998 edition, from the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics, carried 22 interviews.

He kept going back. In 2019 he returned to Johnson Space Center and interviewed ten more astronauts, some retired and some active. Three of them were on the International Space Station while he asked the questions. The campaign page says he has interviewed more than fifty. The interviews he has described on the record are the ones above. The image that matters is the same either way: he was on the ground, and the people with the view were in orbit, answering him.

In a 2012 interview, twenty-five years after the book, he said he had written about the effect and had not been to space to have it. The flight he wanted then was the space station. He also said his purpose was to pass on what other people saw, and that it would be something else entirely to see it himself.

He has already been asked the question this flight raises, which is how far you have to go. After William Shatner's Blue Origin flight, people wrote White to say Shatner had felt it. White's answer was that the flight was short and did not go very far, and that the reports still matched what Edgar Mitchell had told him: the shift is not automatic, and it depends on the person it happens to. Distance matters. Openness matters. A capsule at 25 kilometres is a view of the curve and the black sky, from inside the atmosphere. He will be the one who can say what it was.

## What the community did before anyone signed

The public campaign opened on 12 March 2026. The [terms](/docs/Legal/Overview-Effect-Flight/Overview-Effect-Flight-Terms-and-Conditions) set a goal sized for two suborbital seats, one for Frank and one for a Citizen, and they left the carrier unnamed on purpose. Phase 1 closed on 27 April. The page still describes that round as over $172,000 from 157 contributions, with nothing spent.

Then the search. The campaign page calls it a month of calls with spaceflight providers across three continents. The calls ran in the United States, Europe, and China. The brief published for the June vote counts 12 companies: six stratospheric balloon companies, four suborbital, and two orbital or other.

The brief's findings, still on the [vote page](/overview-path-vote), were blunt.

- A single stratospheric seat for Frank was fundable from more than one provider. If the community had said "book it," the team could have.
- Two seats were out of reach, on a balloon and on a rocket. The original promise was Frank plus a Citizen. Hitting it meant more money, or a partnership that changed the price.
- No stratospheric company in the search had a crewed service in regular operation. Most of them pointed at the following year. A few said they could fly sooner, and did not yet have a human-rated vehicle that had flown.
- Virgin Galactic sent a suborbital contract. The deposit fit the raise. The seat did not. Virgin has not flown a commercial passenger since June 2024, and on 12 November 2026 the company said its next commercial flight is expected early in the second quarter of 2027.
- One orbital conversation produced an offer to fly an avatar of Frank rather than Frank. The brief included it so people would know it had been offered. It was not the recommendation.

## Why this vehicle

Zephalto is the carrier on the reservation. The company is already in the MoonDAO network, at [moondao.com/team/zephalto](/team/zephalto). Its flight is a pressurized capsule under a balloon, from a spaceport in France for the first year, six hours in the air.

On 15 April 2026, in its workshops near Toulouse, Zephalto unveiled Altaïr, which it called its first commercial stratospheric capsule. Les Echos reported the capsule as a carbon-composite shell about 5.5 metres long and 2.7 metres wide, built in Brittany, on the order of two tonnes once passengers and systems are aboard, for four passengers and a pilot. The same article put test flights at the end of summer 2026 and the first commercial flight at the end of 2027. Zephalto's own FAQ, read for this report, does not give Frank a date. This post does not invent one.

What the FAQ does specify is the profile. An hour and a half to climb, three hours at 25 kilometres, an hour and a half back down. They describe the view at the top as the curvature of the Earth, a blue halo, a black sky, and a panorama of more than 1,400 kilometres. They use White's phrase for it. They also say the cabin feels like an airliner: temperate, no weightlessness, and open to anyone who can fly on a commercial plane. Their published fare is €180,000 a guest. This post does not disclose the price on MoonDAO's reservation. The published fare is here so the scale of a seat is on the page next to a community raise of about $165,000.

The two panels are the whole argument for this flight, and the whole argument against pretending it is something else.

On the left, Zephalto sits just above a commercial jet and far below the two lines people use for the word "space." The Kármán line, 100 kilometres, is the line in the original campaign terms: a suborbital seat for Frank was the 50 percent milestone, and two suborbital seats were the full goal. Eighty kilometres is the line the United States has used for astronaut wings. Virgin's earlier spaceplane flights were in that band. Blue Origin's New Shepard flights have gone past 100 kilometres. Altaïr does not.

On the right, the same altitudes reverse. Most of the air is low. At a jet's cruise altitude, about 22 percent of sea-level pressure is still overhead. At 25 kilometres, about 2.5 percent is. Zephalto's "above 98 percent of the atmosphere" and the standard atmosphere are the same claim, within about a point. The black sky and the curve are what that pressure buys. They are also what White has spent forty years asking astronauts to describe.

The row labeled Armstrong is the practical reason the capsule exists. Around 19 kilometres, ambient pressure is about 6.3 kilopascals, the vapor pressure of water at body temperature. An unpressurized person does not have a view from there. Zephalto says the capsule is being built to airliner certification, with a type certificate from EASA still ahead of it, and that the balloon work sits on decades of flights by CNES, the French space agency. Those are their statements about a vehicle that has not yet carried a commercial passenger. They belong in the post because they are the safety case, and because they are not finished.

The reservation is two seats on that flight. Frank's, and the one beside him. ## The seat beside him

One Citizen flies with Frank. The [terms](/docs/Legal/Overview-Effect-Flight/Overview-Effect-Flight-Terms-and-Conditions) already publish the selection, in four rounds. It is not a lottery, a sweepstakes, or a drawing. The terms say so in those words. Receiving $OVERVIEW for a contribution is not the purchase of a chance. The tokens are governance weight. Candidacy is a separate act.

1. **Round 1 is the leaderboard.** Contributors pledge $OVERVIEW to one Citizen. The tokens stay in the contributor's wallet. The 25 Citizens with the most backing go on. The terms say this round closes no later than 14 days after a carrier is secured. This post is not that notice. The closing date will be published on the campaign page.
2. **Round 2 is an essay.** Up to 1,000 words on the Overview Effect, scored on criteria drawn from White's own account of it. The top 10 go on. Advancing Citizens complete identity checks before this round.
3. **Round 3 is an astronaut committee.** A video, then a ranking by professional and commercial astronauts of who should fly with Frank. The top 5 go on.
4. **Round 4 is the community.** Finalists speak in public. vMOONEY holders vote with the staked quadratic vote in the MoonDAO Constitution. One person wins.

To enter as a candidate, a person has to be a MoonDAO Citizen, at least 18, and not Frank, not a member of the Executive Branch, and not someone who has already been to space. They need a passport and the ability to travel to the provider's country. The provider's own health and age rules apply at flight time. Zephalto's public FAQ says a person who can fly commercially can fly with them, with no age or fitness bar beyond that. The terms still govern, and the provider's rules at the time of flight govern. A contribution is not required to compete. Citizenship is.

The $100 path is how a new contributor becomes a Citizen inside the campaign. The Overview Crew tier on the page includes one sponsored year of citizenship, which otherwise has a price on [Join](/join). The same page lists a signed copy of the fourth edition at $1,000, and a private briefing with Frank and the mission team at $10,000. Those are supporter tiers. They are not extra ballots.

## What is settled, and what is not

1. **The carrier is Zephalto, and the reservation is two seats.** Frank's seat, and one beside him for a Citizen. That is the news in this post.
2. **The date is not settled.** Zephalto's published profile is a six-hour flight to 25 kilometres. A newspaper account from the April unveiling put commercial flights at the end of 2027, after a test campaign. Frank's week will be announced when there is one to announce.
3. **The altitude is settled, and it should be said plainly.** Twenty-five kilometres, above nearly all of the air, under the Kármán line, in a balloon rather than a rocket, with no period of weightlessness. The June vote kept a suborbital door open. This reservation walks through the door that was actually open.
4. **The money is settled only in the sense that it has not been spent.** The page said so on 19 November, and showed $165,221 toward $250,000. The dollar figure will move. The milestone on the page has not moved.
5. **The person in the second seat is not settled.** The rounds are published. The closing date of the first round has not been posted. Until it is, the leaderboard is the place to look, and the terms are the rules.

White's 2012 line still fits. He has spent the years since the book passing on a view he had not had. The community that uses his phrase raised the money, ran the search, voted to hold out for two seats, and signed the reservation that gives him one of them. The rest of the work is a date, a second name, and the day the capsule leaves France.
