Frank White gave the Overview Effect its name. It describes the shift astronauts report when they see the whole Earth from above: a planet without borders, held in a layer of atmosphere thinner than seems possible. For nearly 40 years, Frank has interviewed the people who felt it.

He has never felt it himself.

That is about to change. **MoonDAO has secured Frank's seat to near space.** He will fly with [Zephalto](https://www.zephalto.com), in a pressurized capsule lifted by a stratospheric balloon to about 25 kilometres, high enough to see the black sky and the curve of the Earth. We have secured the whole capsule. The seat beside Frank is still open, and the community will choose who fills it.

## The man who named the feeling

In the 1980s, Frank began asking astronauts and cosmonauts a simple question: what was it like to see the Earth from space? Over the following decades he interviewed more than 50 of them. The answers kept pointing to the same experience: awe, a sudden sense of how fragile and connected everything is, and a lasting change in how they saw their place on the planet.

Frank called it the Overview Effect. His book of the same name is now in its fourth edition. He co-founded the Overview Institute, and the term has spread far beyond spaceflight, into documentaries, classrooms and the language of nearly every company that sells a trip upward.

Then he watched everyone else go. Astronauts, then the first private customers, then celebrities. The person who gave the industry its vocabulary for *why* we go was still on the ground.

> Everyone told us no one deserves to go more than Frank. Plenty of people said it. MoonDAO was the only group that actually fought for it.

Frank is not only the mission's passenger. He is a MoonDAO Citizen and a Senator in our governance, and he has worked alongside the community every step of the way, from town halls with astronauts to daily calls during the raise.

## The hard part: finding a ride

Raising the money turned out to be the easy part.

We met with more than 12 providers in China, the US and Europe: six stratospheric balloon companies, four suborbital rocket companies, and two orbital or other options. We judged each one on the same seven questions:

1. Is it ready to fly people?
2. Does it have the regulatory approvals it needs?
3. How safe is the vehicle's design?
4. Does the company have the funding to get to its first flights?
5. Is its schedule realistic?
6. Are the contract terms fair to backers?
7. What is the total cost for Frank and a second flyer?

## Why Zephalto

We chose Zephalto to take Frank to the edge of space.

Zephalto is a French company building low-carbon flights to the stratosphere. Its pressurized Céleste capsule hangs beneath a large balloon and rises gently, with no rocket burn and no high g-forces, to about 25 kilometres. That is more than twice the cruising height of an airliner and above roughly 97% of the atmosphere. From there the sky turns black in daylight, and the Earth's curve and the thin blue line of the atmosphere are plain to see.

It is not the Kármán line, and we won't pretend it is. But the Overview Effect is not about crossing a line. It is about *seeing*: the planet as a whole, the fragile layer of air around it, and the dark beyond. In Zephalto's capsule, Frank will have hours to look.

**Who can enter?** Any MoonDAO Citizen who:

- is 18 or older and has not been to space,
- holds a valid passport and can travel to the launch site,
- can pass Zephalto's health requirements at flight time, and
- completes KYC if they reach Round 2.

Frank and members of MoonDAO's Executive Branch cannot enter. If the chosen candidate can't fly for medical reasons, the seat goes to the next-ranked finalist.

**You don't have to pay to compete.** Back the mission with $100 or more and your MoonDAO Citizenship is included at no extra cost. Existing Citizens can enter without contributing.

## Questions

<details>
<summary>Is 25 km really space?</summary>

No. The Kármán line, the conventional boundary of space, is at 100 km. At about 25 km, Frank will be above roughly 97% of the atmosphere, under a black sky, looking at the curve of the Earth. That's why we call it near space. We chose this flight for what Frank will see and for how long he can see it, not for crossing a line on a chart.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Why not a suborbital rocket?</summary>

We met with suborbital providers. A rocket seat crosses 100 km but gives a few minutes of view, and seats for two were far beyond what the raise could cover. Others were not yet ready to fly people. A stratospheric flight gives Frank hours at altitude, and it let the community secure a whole capsule.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Has any money been spent?</summary>

Not on the round-one funds. Every dollar from round one is still held. Payouts follow the fund split set out in the Overview Effect Flight Terms.

</details>

<details>
<summary>I already backed the mission. Do I need to do anything?</summary>

No. If you contributed $100 or more, you can claim your Citizenship and enter the selection for the open seat. Your $OVERVIEW tokens also count as backing in Round 1.
