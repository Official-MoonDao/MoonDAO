## Bite-Sized Space News

## SpaceX Reuse Record

Last week, SpaceX achieved a new record by successfully launching and landing a Falcon 9 rocket for its unprecedented 18th mission. The rocket carried 23 Starlink internet satellites into orbit from Florida's Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The first stage of the Falcon 9 returned to Earth and made a vertical landing on a drone ship in the Atlantic Ocean.

SpaceX's emphasis on rocket reusability is a crucial aspect of their approach to spaceflight and enables ambitious exploration plans, including Mars settlement. The company has been actively building its Starlink broadband megaconstellation, with nearly 5,000 operational satellites in low Earth orbit (LEO). The rocket that set the new record had already participated in 12 previous Starlink missions.

[Check out the landing video here.](https://twitter.com/SpaceX/status/1720603360154521898)

## Sierra Space’s Tenacity

Last Thursday, Sierra Space unveiled its first Dream Chaser space plane, named "Tenacity." The Colorado-based company plans to ship the space plane to NASA's Neil A. Armstrong Test Facility in Ohio for environmental testing. Sierra Space holds a NASA contract for robotic resupply missions to the International Space Station (ISS) with the Dream Chaser, and Tenacity is set to be the first to visit the ISS on a test flight potentially as early as April 2024.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/abSn9bKRkkED19ycbmv1Da)

The Dream Chaser combines aesthetics and functionality, designed to endure reentry temperatures over 3,000 degrees Fahrenheit while remaining cool to the touch minutes after landing. Sierra Space also envisions using the vehicle for future crewed missions.
