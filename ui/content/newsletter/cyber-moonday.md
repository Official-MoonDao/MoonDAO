## Bite-Sized Space News

## 10 Million Mile Laser

NASA's Psyche mission achieved a groundbreaking milestone by deploying the Deep Space Optical Communications technology demonstration (DSOC), showcasing the most distant laser communications experiment to date. As the Psyche spacecraft embarks on a journey to explore a metal asteroid between Mars and Jupiter, DSOC is conducting a unique experiment aiming to revolutionize data transmission in space.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/uFBx2ExgCq8VxwQNNdPemj)

This technology, leveraging an invisible near-infrared laser, holds the potential to transmit data at speeds 10 to 100 times faster than conventional radio wave systems, potentially shaping future communications for Mars missions. DSOC recently accomplished its "first light," successfully transmitting and receiving data, marking a pivotal step towards high-data-rate communications and paving the way for humanity's deeper exploration of space. The precision and capabilities demonstrated by DSOC across millions of miles represent a crucial leap in advancing optical communication for future space missions, offering prospects for enhanced scientific discoveries and explorations beyond Earth.

As Psyche continues its voyage toward a metal asteroid situated between Mars and Jupiter, the spacecraft's ambitious journey, spanning 2.2 billion miles over six years, remains on track. Notably, the recent DSOC experiment transmitted encoded laser data from nearly 10 million miles away, successfully received by the Hale Telescope in Pasadena, California.

Despite this impressive feat, it's important to note that the average distance between Earth and Mars is around 140 million miles, indicating that this milestone represents only a fraction of the vast distances that future Mars-Earth communications will need to cover. But this achievement lays the groundwork for the continued advancement of communication technologies over considerable interplanetary distances, and sets the stage for even more ambitious space exploration endeavors in the future.

## Most Powerful Cosmic Ray in Decades

Researchers at the Telescope Array in Utah have detected an extraordinarily powerful cosmic ray, marking a significant outer space mystery. This ultra-high-energy particle, tentatively named the "Amaterasu particle," likely originated from beyond the Milky Way, yet its precise source remains enigmatic. Scientists speculate that unknown physics might have generated this particle, challenging conventional understanding.

With an estimated energy of 240 exa-electron volts (EeV), this finding defies theoretical limits and parallels the enigmatic "Oh-My-God" particle discovered over three decades ago, pushing beyond what should be feasible for cosmic rays. The Telescope Array's upgraded sensitivity aims to unravel these cosmic riddles, offering hope for tracing the origins of these incredibly high-energy cosmic phenomena.
