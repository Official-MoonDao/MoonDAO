## Contribute to DeSci with MoonDAO

**Looking for a great way to start contributing to a MoonDAO project, advancing the cause of open science, and earn your first rewards for work done?** Get started with the recently approved [DeSci Community Validations project (MDP-129)](https://vote.moondao.com/#/proposal/0x235a457691bfa1f7c53313e2a1606a882301969207f3670e1a369681cfe3bc7c).

Community Validations are a DAO-native way to build reputation by validating science in an open and transparent fashion. MoonDAO members can easily contribute, get credited, and receive rewards for their contributions to a [collection of DeSci Nodes](https://nodes.desci.com/community/moondao). Members can contribute by publishing and submitting research for curation, or by validating and curating submitted work.

The [unified, open and decentralized infrastructure](http://codex.desci.com/) underpinning Community Validations ensures that contributions can build into a collective reputation over time, helping showcase both the individual work of a contributor and the collective efforts of a DAO.

MoonDAO has allocated 1ETH to reward authors and validators for their contributions, as well as quarterly $MOONEY and potential retroactive rewards.

[Read more about how you can help the Community Validations](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1VyZmXK-4HDNDfbXsJrzBx2xLd_SkEH-0aLAA-WEA9Vg/edit) project or reach out to Phil on Discord to answer any questions you might have about getting involved.

## Crew 8 Launch

After a Friday postponement and a Saturday night scrub, the Crew-8 team launched late Sunday night on a Falcon 9 rocket from Kennedy Space Center to the International Space Station. Saturday night's unsuccessful launch attempt was postponed "due to unfavorable conditions in the flight path of the SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket and Dragon spacecraft."

The Crew-8 launch carried NASA astronauts Matthew Dominick, Michael Barratt, and Jeanette Epps, as well as Roscosmos cosmonaut Alexander Grebenkin to the orbital laboratory. The targeted docking time is about 3 a.m. on Tuesday.

## Starship Wet Dress Rehearsal

SpaceX's next Starship, poised for its third launch attempt, successfully completed a crucial fueling test and wet dress rehearsal yesterday, marking an important milestone toward an impending launch for the world's largest rocket's journey. The stainless-steel behemoth, comprising the Starship rocket and its Super Heavy booster, underwent a rehearsal at SpaceX's Starbase near Boca Chica Beach, Texas, where it was filled with over 10 million pounds of liquid methane and oxygen propellant.

As SpaceX continues to push the boundaries of space travel, the space community eagerly awaits the next milestone in this groundbreaking journey. We are coming close to a third orbital launch attempt for this ambitious next generation vehicle.

## Good Night, Odysseus

With the Sun no longer illuminating the panel that was keeping Odysseus alive in its fallen position on the Moon, Intuitive Machines put the spacecraft in sleep mode on February 29. Before its power was depleted, Odysseus completed a final farewell transmission with this photo of the Moon.

<figure>

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/ptfRreM3VJ7P5kPvsh9upL)

<figcaption>

Credit Intuitive Machines: "Showcases the crescent Earth in the backdrop, a subtle reminder of humanity’s presence in the universe.'

</figcaption>

</figure>

Despite experiencing a landing mishap that left the spacecraft sideways, the mission has been deemd an “unqualified success” by both NASA and Intuitive Machines, one that “met very high level mission objectives to touch down softly on the Moon—softly and safely—and return scientific data.”

The Nova-C lander, named Odysseus, delivered scientific data from nearly all payloads after touching down near the moon's south polar regions. While the landing was not as soft as hoped due to issues with laser rangefinders, the mission accomplished these key objectives.
