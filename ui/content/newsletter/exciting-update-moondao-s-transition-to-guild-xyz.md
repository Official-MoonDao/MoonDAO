## Bite-Sized Space News

## Out of This World Sample Return

In a monumental achievement, a NASA mission successfully returned a canister to Earth containing pristine asteroid rocks from a rocket that was launched *over seven years ago*. These ancient black pebbles and dirt, older than Earth itself, are remnants of the solar system's early days. The rocks, part of an asteroid named Bennu, offer uncontaminated insight into the early chemistry that may have contributed to the emergence of life.

The mission launched on September 8th, 2016, rendezvoused with Bennu two years later, spent two years finding a suitable landing and extraction site, landed on Bennu in October 2020, and then departed in May 2021, to *finally* return the canister and safely land in the Utah desert last week.

The return capsule entered Earth's atmosphere at extreme speeds but deployed parachutes successfully, ensuring its safe landing, despite an altitude misfire for the parachute. Scientists eagerly anticipate analyzing the asteroid rock to uncover its secrets and potential contributions to our understanding of the cosmos and life's origins. This remarkable achievement marks a pivotal moment in space exploration, and researchers are gearing up for a busy week ahead as they delve into the treasure trove of asteroid material.

## SpaceX Reusability Records

It seems like just a couple weeks ago we brought up (yet another) record being broken by SpaceX (last time it was for number of launches in a calendar year)… Well, here’s another one for the record books: the first Falcon 9 rocket to complete its 17th successful launch and landing. As if that wasn’t impressive enough, just four days later, they did it again when another Falcon 9 rocket tied the record for 17 launches, showcasing SpaceX's ability to continuously set and break its own records. SpaceX has rated Falcon 9 for at least 20 launches per booster which we are quickly approaching.

The most recent Falcon 9 launch marked the 200th overall reuse of a booster. This remarkable achievement adds to the company's track record, with a total of 228 successful recoveries since its first success in December 2015. The streak of successful landings has been unbroken since February 2021, with 153 consecutive attempts ending triumphantly.

For the Space Coast, this launch represents the 51st of the year, inching closer to the record of 57 set in 2022. SpaceX's consistent successes and commitment to reusability are shaping the future of space exploration, making it more accessible and efficient than ever before.

The launch also added to SpaceX's impressive overall count, marking the 266th launch since the inaugural Falcon 9 success in 2008 and the 67th this year. This remarkable track record underscores the reliability and reusability of the Falcon 9 rocket. It's important to note that the Falcon 9 is only partially reusable, and SpaceX continues to strive for full and rapid reusability with Starship which is still awaiting review and clearance by the Fish and Wildlife Service (FWS) of changes made to the launchpad, including the water deluge system, before a second launch can happen. This review could take between 30 and 135 days.

**Space Trivia Answer: A) Perseverance.** Perseverance, a car-sized Mars rover, landed on Mars on February 18, 2021. Since then, Perseverance has been actively exploring Mars for 923 sols (949 Earth days). It also transported the mini-helicopter Ingenuity, which achieved the historic first powered flight on another planet, and has since completed 59 successful flights, setting records for speed, distance, and altitude. Perseverance's mission objectives encompass identifying ancient Martian environments that could have supported life, searching for evidence of past microbial life, collecting rock and soil samples, and testing oxygen production from the Martian atmosphere for future human missions.
