## Bite-Sized Space News

## SpaceX Breaks Their Own Record

SpaceX has already made significant strides in the realm of space launches this year. In 2023 alone, they executed an impressive number of launches and marked another historic feat in the process with their 62nd orbital launch this calendar year. The mission broke the orbital launch record that SpaceX also set when it launched a total of 61 orbital missions in 2022.

Looking ahead, with nearly four months left this year, SpaceX has plans for another estimated 20+ launches before the year's conclusion.

To provide context, SpaceX took an entire decade, from 2010 to 2020, to achieve a total of 85 launches. Now, they're on course to potentially reach the same milestone within a single year, thanks to an average cadence of an orbital rocket launch every four days.

[On X, Elon Musk stated](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1698045247547212093) that “*SpaceX has delivered \~80% of all Earth payload mass to orbit in 2023. China is \~10% &amp; rest of world other \~10%*,” and that they are “*aiming for 10 Falcon flights in a month by end of this year, then 12 per month next year*.”

While managing this high launch rate, SpaceX is also actively progressing on their ambitious Starship project in Texas (as we covered last week), demonstrating their unwavering dedication to advancing space technology and exploration which will, if successful, completely revolutionize the launch sector and space economy.

## Crew 6 Return

After the recent docking of Crew 7 to the International Space Station, four astronauts, Stephen Bowen and Warren "Woody" Hoburg of NASA, Sultan Al-Neyadi of the UAE, and Andrey Fedyaev of Russia's Roscosmos, forming Crew-6, have safely returned to Earth following a 184-day mission aboard the ISS. Despite poor weather conditions extending their stay in orbit, the crew landed safely in the Atlantic Ocean off the coast of Jacksonville, Florida.

During their mission on the ISS, the crew accomplished a variety of tasks, including cargo vehicle missions, welcoming a visiting Axiom crew on board, and performing hundreds of scientific experiments for the benefit of all humanity. They also conducted three spacewalks, performed maintenance, and left the space station in an improved state. For astronaut Sultan Al-Neyadi, the flight was historic as the first long-duration expedition by an Arab and Emirati astronaut, and it represented an opportunity to share enthusiasm for space exploration in the Middle East.

The return of Crew-6 sets the stage for Expedition 70, which will begin when Russia's Soyuz MS-23 spacecraft departs the station later this month with Prokopyev, Petelin and Rubio aboard. NASA astronaut Frank Rubio is set to establish a new U.S. record for time on a single space mission at 371 days. Crew-6 was SpaceX's sixth crew rotation flight for NASA and marked the fourth flight of the Dragon spacecraft "Endeavour," which previously flew the Demo-2, Crew-2, and Axiom-1 missions to and from the ISS.

## Latest from ISRO on the Moon

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) has placed the Chandrayaan 3 Moon lander Vikram and the Pragyan rover in sleep mode, shutting down their active instruments, including the thermal probe, seismometer, and plasma experiment.

The Pragyan rover, having covered 100 meters on the lunar surface over ten Earth days, entered sleep mode on September 2nd, positioning its solar panel for the next local sunrise when it is hoped that it will spring back to life when light reaches it again on September 22nd. If the lander and rover survive the extreme lunar night's sub-zero temperatures, ISRO aims to reestablish communication with them for further tasks, although the mission has already completed their primary goals.

Additionally, ISRO surprised observers when it successfully performed a second soft landing via a "hop experiment". The lander lifted approximately 40 centimeters and safely landed 30-40 centimeters away from its previous position, where the rover had been deployed. This hop demonstrated reeling and redeployment mechanisms for the lander's instruments, which were folded back before the maneuver and redeployed after the hop.

While the hop could have affected the rover's equipment and the lander's instruments due to lunar dust displacement, it serves to inspire future sample return and human missions. ISRO shared preliminary data from Chandrayaan 3's experiments, with in-depth scientific results expected later.
