## Space News

**Green Comet:** The “Green Comet” C/2022 E3 (ZTF) is rapidly approaching perigee, its closest point to Earth, making it easier to spot in the night sky. The comet was first discovered by astronomers using the Zwicky Transient Facility (ZTF) at the Palomar Observatory in California in March 2022. It is expected to reach its brightest magnitude in early February as it approaches perigee on February 1st. The comet could be visible to the naked eye under the right dark sky conditions, but binoculars or a telescope will make it easier to spot. It's located in the northern skies and can be seen just after sunset as a faint greenish glow.

**Starship:** SpaceX is nearing the critical test phase of its launch campaign for its massive Starship vehicle, and today the company is conducting a "wet dress rehearsal" test at its launch site in South Texas in Boca Chica. This test is essential to demonstrate the safe fueling of so much propellant at once and it is one of the biggest milestones remaining ahead of an orbital launch attempt which could take place in a month or two. The wet dress rehearsal is a key part of the test campaign to ensure the rocket clears the launch site so that it does not damage valuable infrastructure, SpaceX is proceeding more cautiously toward its orbital test flight. If the test goes well, the next step is expected to be a full static firing of all 33 Raptor engines on Booster 7.

**Space Trivia Answer: B) Chang'e** is the Chinese goddess associated with the Moon, she is said to live on the Moon after she stole the elixir of immortality from her husband, Houyi the archer ([read more](https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Chinese_Stories/Houyi_and_Chang%27e)). Chang'e is also the name of a series of lunar exploration missions launched by the China National Space Administration, named after the goddess. The program began in 2007 with the launch of Chang'e 1, which was followed by Chang'e 2 &amp; 3, and Chang'e 4 became the first spacecraft to land on the far side of the Moon in 2019 and Chang'e 5, which successfully collected and returned samples from the lunar surface in 2020.

These missions have provided valuable scientific data and images of the Moon, and have also demonstrated China's growing capabilities in space exploration. In the future, China plans to continue the Chang'e program with the launch of Chang'e 6, 7, and 8, which will focus on the study of the lunar polar regions, the construction of a lunar research station, and the development of lunar resources.
