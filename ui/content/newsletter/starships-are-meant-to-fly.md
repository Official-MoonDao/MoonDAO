## Starship Second Test Flight

SpaceX's monumental Starship second test flight launched early on Saturday morning and it still has the entire space industry abuzz with excitement... This 2nd launch has been the most hyped launch since... Well, since the very first test flight on April 20th. That first 4/20 launch resulted in some pretty extensive damages to the Orbital Launch Mount (OLM) and caused a lot of doubt about whether SpaceX would even be able to attempt another launch in 2023 (although the real delay ended up being approval from the Fish and Wildlife Service).

But SpaceX moved with surprising speed and made significant changes to the launch mount, including the addition of the rocket "bidet" and water cooled steel plate, to withstand the forces exerted by the world’s most powerful rocket with 33 Raptor engines, which all ignited successfully.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/bM6U4QzPghvJPuXuWoMtb9)

During the test flight, the stage separation occurred as planned, highlighting some HUGE advancements from the previous test flight which had failed to separate. The biggest challenge for this launch was the introduction of a new hot staging ring that separates the two stages and allows the ignition of the upper stage while the first stage continues burning, a maneuver that has never been attempted on a rocket of this size.

The first stage Super Heavy booster experienced a "Rapid Unscheduled Disassembly" (RUD) soon after separation, possibly due to fuel sloshing on the quick flip and burn back maneuver, while the second stage continued on its mission toward Hawaii before also experiencing an anomaly and RUD. While information is still forthcoming about what exactly happened, SpaceX views this test as a major success, attaining higher altitudes compared to its first launch, and gathering more crucial data for future missions.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/gswzhBY95MrcKh96CdX1RW)

SpaceX aims to refine its hot staging strategy and hardware for subsequent flights, crucial for endeavors like NASA's Artemis mission to the moon. [Elon Musk has stated that a third flight could be ready in 3-4 weeks](https://twitter.com/elonmusk/status/1726422074254578012), although he is often overly optimistic in his timelines. Elon also shared that the powerful launch did not cause any significant damage to the pad which bodes well for a quicker turnaround for the third launch.

While much of the mainstream media has portrayed the launch and explosion as a failure, most people within the industry see this test launch as a major success that offers a great deal of insight and things to learn for future improvement. Eric Berger wrote an excellent and lengthy rebuttal article to the narrative that this launch was a failure.

**Read: “**[**Sorry doubters, Starship actually had a remarkably successful flight**](https://arstechnica.com/space/2023/11/heres-why-this-weekends-starship-launch-was-actually-a-huge-success/)**”**

Starship represents SpaceX's vision for interplanetary travel, with full reusability as its cornerstone. The rocket's potential for rapid reuse would redefine space travel, although challenges remain in ensuring its reliability.

## CAPSTONE One-Year

Advanced Space celebrates a significant milestone as their CAPSTONE mission completes a successful year orbiting the Moon, marking over 365 days of uninterrupted operations in the cislunar space.

During this tenure, CAPSTONE maintained its unique Near Rectilinear Halo Orbit (NRHO), aiding in data collection for future lunar Gateway space station operations in support of NASA's Artemis program.

Additionally, the mission showcased pioneering spacecraft-to-spacecraft navigation technology, the Cislunar Autonomous Positioning System (CAPS), and tested the first-ever onboard Chip Scale Atomic Clock at the Moon.

With around 50% of fuel remaining, the company is engaging with partners and the community for further experiments, expanding the mission's scope beyond its primary customer, NASA.

Improving communications and timekeeping in the Moon will be critical to a future lunar settlement and is something that MoonDAO is actively working to facilitate in a decentralized manner.
