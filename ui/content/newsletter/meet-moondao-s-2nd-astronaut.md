## Bite-Sized Space News

## Soyuz Docking

Following an initial launch abort, the Soyuz MS-25 spacecraft successfully transported NASA astronaut Tracy Dyson, Russian cosmonaut Oleg Novitsky, and Belarusian astronaut Marina Vasilevskaya to the International Space Station (ISS).

The launch from Kazakhstan's Baikonur facility occurred after a two-day delay due to an automatic safety system interruption. Yuri Borisov, head of the Russian space agency, attributed the abort to a voltage drop in a power source.

Dyson, on her third ISS mission, will stay for six months, while Novitsky, embarking on his fourth flight, and Vasilevskaya, on her debut mission, will spend 12 days aboard. The station remains a potent symbol of international cooperation despite tensions between Russia and the West and plans to remain operational until 2030.

## Starship Static Fire

SpaceX's upcoming Starship test flight has taken a fiery step forward with a recent static fire test. The Starship upper stage, equipped with all six Raptor engines, roared to life during the full-duration test at SpaceX's Starbase site in South Texas on March 25. This important prelaunch check, where engines are ignited while the vehicle remains anchored, sets the stage for the fourth Starship test flight anticipated in early May.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/4wZjhvKg43ohmvoMPtzJSs)

[**Check out a video of the static fire.**](https://twitter.com/SpaceX/status/1772372482214801754)

Despite challenges encountered during previous missions, such as the booster's unsuccessful landing burn and the craft's breakup upon reentry, notable successes were achieved, including flawless ascent burns and the opening and closing of the payload door. SpaceX is ambitiously planning six or more test flights this year, pending regulatory approvals from the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration, which is currently investigating the events of Starship's third flight.
