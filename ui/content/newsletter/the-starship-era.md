## Bite-Sized Space News

## Starship Takes Flight

Starship Super Heavy took flight on the meme-worthy date of April 20th. The world’s largest and most powerful rocket cleared the pad and passed Max-Q, but failed to achieve stage separation and second stage ignition.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/nX5iYahfQ2SzteiRUnF6HF)

SpaceX and Elon Musk had been working to manage expectations for this launch and publicly stated that just clearing the pad was the primary objective and everything else is icing on the cake -- they basically wanted to gather as much data and information as possible for future iterations. While the rocket did clear the pad, it also left behind some super heavy damage to the Orbital Launch Mount (OLM) and overall Stage Zero infrastructure, likely much more than anticipated by SpaceX.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/xhVJ3cojtr2TnveFpSCWGy)

While analysis is still ongoing, early word indicates that the force of the engines may have shattered the concrete, rather than simply eroding it, sending large chunks of concrete flying like shrapnel, possibly damaging some of the Raptor engines which may be have been a contributing factor to the number of engines that failed.

The long-term ramifications of this are still unknown, but it is expected that SpaceX will have to make significant changes to Stage Zero before they will be able to attempt a second launch of Super Heavy.

Excitement was guaranteed, and it was definitely delivered.
