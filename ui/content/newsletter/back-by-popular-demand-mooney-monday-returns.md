## Second Blue Origin Astronaut Update

Our year-long journey to bring our second astronaut, Yan KeJun, into the United States for astronaut training has been fraught with challenges. From a pool of over 8,000 entries, Yan KeJun, a father of three from China, was randomly selected via our Ticket To Space Sweepstakes to journey into sub-orbital space with Blue Origin.

We planned for his arrival in the U.S. well ahead of his scheduled flight for astronaut training. This preparation was crucial to ensure that Yan met Blue Origin’s functional prerequisites for space travel. Unfortunately, on three separate attempts, the U.S. Government denied his application for a travel visa.

As a result, Blue Origin requested that we move on to another candidate, and we were unable to successfully contact our runner-up candidates drawn during the initial selection. As a Decentralized Autonomous Organization (DAO), we will navigate this situation as a community, focusing on our next steps for how we move forward from here. MoonDAO remains firmly committed to decentralizing access to space, regardless of one's nationality or financial status. We stand resolute in our mission to continue promoting these principles and ideals as we work together to navigate our way forward.

## Bite-Sized Space News

## Finally, a Lunar Landing!

After a string of recent failures to land on the Moon, including Japan’s attempt earlier this year via ispace and the failure of Russia’s Luna-25 mission just a few weeks ago, we finally have some good news to report when it comes to lunar activity! India has landed its Chandrayaan-3 spacecraft on the moon, becoming only the fourth nation ever to accomplish such a feat!

Chandrayaan-3 launched on July 14, 2023, from the Satish Dhawan Space Center to demonstrate new technologies and achieve India's inaugural soft landing on another celestial body. Now, after more than a month in transit and a few nail-biting moments during descent, India's space prowess shines brighter as Chandrayaan-3 made history by successfully landing near the moon's unexplored south pole.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/u6UemzxbdNsKSrMZ3xbMFk)

The triumphant touchdown sparked jubilation across India and earned accolades around the world, with many dubbing it India's most significant scientific milestone. The rover, equipped with instruments for chemical composition experiments and robotic path planning, is set to enrich our understanding of the lunar landscape, particularly near the south pole which is expected to be the center of any future human presence on the Moon.

With a budget of approximately $75 million, Chandrayaan-3's success underscores India's prowess in cost-effective space engineering. This achievement holds immense significance as it advances India's space ambitions and stands as a source of national pride.

Chandrayaan-3's success resonates as a beacon of exploration and innovation that unites us all and paves the way toward humanity's future on the Moon and beyond, and MoonDAO couldn't be more excited for what's to come!

## Crew-7 Launch &amp; Arrival

SpaceX launched a diverse crew of international astronauts toward the International Space Station (ISS) early in the morning of Aug. 26. The four astronauts of the Crew-7 mission lifted off atop a brand-new SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket from Pad 39A from Kennedy Space Center. The Crew Dragon capsule "Endurance" successfully docked at the ISS nearly 30 hours later and these astronauts are now settling into life aboard the space station.

Crew-7's significance lies in its diverse crew composition. Composed of members from NASA, ESA, Roscosmos, and JAXA, this international quartet is the first-ever all-international crew to fly aboard a Dragon capsule. Their mission, SpaceX's seventh operational commercial crew flight for NASA, exemplifies the global nature of space exploration.

As the crew embarks on a six-month stay aboard the ISS, they will take over from the astronauts of NASA's Crew-6 mission. This mission also marks several significant firsts, including U.S. Marine Corps Lieutenant Colonel Jasmin Moghbeli becoming the second Iranian-American to fly in space. The journey is a maiden voyage for Konstantin Borisov of Roscosmos, and Morgensen, the first European to pilot a SpaceX Dragon capsule.

## Starship Launching Again Soon?

Last Friday, SpaceX performed a successful second static fire test of Booster 9, marking a significant step toward an upcoming launch of its mammoth fully stacked Starship rocket. The test involved all 33 Raptor engines igniting, with only two engines cutting out and running shorter than the intended six-second duration.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/jwYokfJHdzXmhosTk9t9Kh)

The first static fire test in early August saw four Raptor engines failing to ignite and the static fire was shut down prematurely. A static fire is a major step toward advancing to the second orbital flight test of Starship, which could happen as early as September, although there are still a few hurdles to pass, including regulatory approval from the FAA following the failed separation of the first test flight.

SpaceX is attempting to address the stage separation issue by adding a "hot staging ring" to facilitate this challenging maneuver during flight. That is but one of many changes that SpaceX has made with everything they learned from the first flight. As you may recall, Stage Zero and the Orbital Launch Mount received pretty significant damage with some naysayers claiming that it would be a full year before SpaceX could repair the damage and even start thinking about a second test flight.

However, SpaceX moved quickly to repair the damage to the underlying concrete pad and also made some changes to the OLM, including a massive water-cooled steel plate and deluge system which has been the focus of recent tests, including this full-duration static test fire. It's safe to say that all eyes are again looking toward Boca Chica for the highly anticipated second flight.
