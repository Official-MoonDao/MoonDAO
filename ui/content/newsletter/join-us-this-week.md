## Bite-Sized Space News

## Chandrayaan Won’t Wake Up

Engineers at the Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO) are still making efforts to revive the Chandrayaan-3 lunar lander and rover after a planned two-week hibernation during the long lunar night. Attempts to establish communication with the Vikram lander and Pragyan rover have thus far been unsuccessful. ISRO hoped the batteries would recharge and the modules would reawaken when the Sun rose around September 22nd.

Chandrayaan-3 successfully landed near the lunar south pole in August, making India the fourth nation to achieve a lunar landing. During their operational phase, Pragyan explored the landing site and transmitted images, while Vikram conducted scientific experiments, including analyzing lunar dust for traces of sulfur. Although the mission completed its primary goals, ISRO hopes the spacecraft may have survived the lunar night, and efforts continue to establish communication, although the odds are minimal.

## Things are Getting Heavy

SpaceX successfully conducted a "static fire" test of its Falcon Heavy rocket at NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida, in preparation for the launch of NASA's Psyche asteroid mission scheduled for next week on October 12. During the test, the rocket's 27 first-stage engines were briefly ignited while it remained anchored to the launch pad.

The Psyche mission aims to explore the asteroid Psyche, located in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter, with the probe expected to reach it in 2029 and study it from orbit for at least 26 months. Falcon Heavy, the second-most powerful rocket currently in operation, will be used for the first time on a NASA mission after seven previous flights.

## Like a Virgin

Virgin Galactic is also gearing up to launch another sub-orbital flight with three paying passengers aboard. The flight was delayed by one day and is now set for October 6th from the New Mexico desert. Virgin has gotten into a rhythm of launching about once per month as of late. Blue Origin is expected to send up an uncrewed launch of their New Shepard rocket in October as well. With both entities performing regular commercial launches, we could see the total number of individuals reaching space to *double* in the next 10 years compared to the past 60 years.
