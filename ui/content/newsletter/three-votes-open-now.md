## Bite-Sized Space News

## New Crew Access Arm

SpaceX recently installed a new Crew Access Arm at Launch Complex 40 in Florida, which marks a significant step in managing the growing launch cadence on the Space Coast. The purpose of this tower is to alleviate the load on LC-39A, the primary site for crewed launches in the United States.

Despite being under construction, Space Launch Complex 40 remains the world's busiest launch site, with 46 launches this year alone.

Due to the busy schedule at Launch Complex 39A, SpaceX is considering utilizing SLC-40 for the [Axiom-3 mission](https://www.axiomspace.com/missions/ax3), potentially marking the first crewed launch from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in over five decades. These developments align with SpaceX's ambitious goal of exceeding 100 launches in 2024.

## ISS Resupply Mission

On Saturday, a SpaceX Dragon cargo capsule successfully docked with the International Space Station, delivering a substantial load of fresh supplies for the station's astronaut crew. Launched atop a Falcon 9 rocket from Kennedy Space Center’s LC-39A on November 9, the mission, known as CRS-29, marked SpaceX's 29th cargo mission for NASA.

The Dragon spacecraft reached the ISS with over 6,500 pounds (2,950 kilograms) of supplies and scientific equipment, including NASA's Atmospheric Waves Experiment (AWE) to study gravity waves in Earth's atmosphere and the ILLUMA-T project for testing high-speed laser communications in orbit, this successful mission contributes to the ongoing collaborative efforts between SpaceX and NASA in advancing scientific research and technology in space.
