## Bite-Sized Space News

## Super Heavy Static Fire

Last week, all eyes were on Boca Chica, Texas after SpaceX COO Gwynne Shotwell unexpectedly announced that they’d be performing a 33-engine static fire test of the Super Heavy booster. This milestone marked one of the most anticipated tests left to do prior a fully stacked orbital flight test of what will be the world’s most powerful rocket.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/7vXDEn6ATeKTvppMy78qco)

31 of the 33 engines fired at 50% thrust which was still enough to be able to reach orbit according to Elon Musk. According to Musk, SpaceX is on track for the orbital flight test next month in March, and while he has a tendency to give highly optimistic dates and timeframes, it is starting to feel much more real after this most recent test.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/kd5a2Zq9DmZPbKFVScUEBR)

During the announcement, Shotwell also said: “Keep in mind, this first one is really a test flight... and the real goal is to not blow up the launch pad, that is success.”

## Saudi Arabia’s Astronauts

More and more countries are striving to play a bigger role in the burgeoning space industry, and Saudi Arabia recently announced the selection of the two astronauts who will fly to the ISS this spring aboard the private AX-2 Axiom space mission.

Rayyanah Barnawi and Ali Alqarni will be only the second and third Saudi citizens to fly to space, and Barnawi will be the first Saudi woman. They will join the mission’s commander, former NASA astronaut Peggy Whitson, and a customer, John Shoffner.

Saudi Arabia is also a signatory of the Artemis Accords and while their government may leave much to criticize, it is great to see them embracing space and opening opportunities for men and women alike. We will be seeing an increasing number of private citizens traveling beyond the Karman Line and countries without established space programs will probably serve as some of the first early customers as this announcement shows.
