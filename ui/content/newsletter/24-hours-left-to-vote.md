## Blue Origin Returning to Action?

According to [Eric Berger from Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/space/2023/09/a-year-after-new-shepards-accident-blue-origin-may-return-to-flight-next-month/), Blue Origin is gearing up for a return to flight with its New Shepard rocket after a year-long hiatus following an accident. According to Eric’s inside sources, the company is planning an uncrewed test flight in early October, with a crewed mission expected in mid-February next year, marking its first crewed mission since August 2022.

The accident occurred a year ago when Blue Origin’s NS-23 rocket exploded above the West Texas desert. Fortunately, there were no passengers aboard the New Shepard capsule, which safely landed after triggering the launch escape system.

An investigation revealed that the rocket engine was operating at higher temperatures than designed, possibly due to design changes made during previous flights of the booster. Blue Origin has since implemented corrective actions, including design changes to the combustion chamber and operating parameters, to address the issue.

The company has not officially provided updates on its return to flight plans, but Eric’s sources indicate that preparations are underway. It remains unclear which rocket Blue Origin will use for the return, as it has been developing newer boosters based on lessons learned from the accident in order to improve the safety and reliability of the booster.

Blue Origin faces renewed competition from Virgin Galactic, which has been demonstrating an impressive cadence of human spaceflights with its VSS Unity spacecraft. Virgin Galactic plans to continue flying humans on a monthly cadence (with the next one scheduled for October 5th), while Blue Origin had been averaging one human spaceflight every two months before the accident.

Blue Origin’s return to action with New Shepard bodes well for MoonDAO’s impending second astronaut reselection to identify who from our community will be flying aboard the rocket in the near future, serving as MoonDAO’s successor to [Coby Cotton who flew aboard NS-22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YXXlSG-du7c).

## Inspiration4 Anniversary

The recent two-year anniversary of Inspiration4, the groundbreaking all-private crewed mission to Earth orbit, celebrated its historic journey that took four civilians into space using a SpaceX Dragon capsule. The mission attracted global attention for its trailblazing nature, diverse crew, and the remarkable achievement of raising $250 million for St. Jude's Children's Research Hospital. On September 15, 2021, the mission lifted off, marking the beginning of a new era in space exploration.

Inspiration4, led by billionaire Jared Isaacman, featured a diverse crew, including analog astronaut Sian Proctor, the first Black woman pilot in space, Hayley Arceneaux, a pediatric cancer survivor and the first astronaut with an artificial prosthesis, and U.S. Air Force veteran Christopher Sembroski. Proctor and Arceneaux have released books about their experiences, and Sembroski has joined Blue Origin as an avionics engineer. Jared is now in training for a new series of missions under the Polaris Program, which also supports St. Jude's. Isaacman has a series of three future missions planned with SpaceX, culminating in the first crewed mission of Starship. The first of these missions, Polaris Dawn, is scheduled to launch early next year.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/cDUQzNsVWHnzWL9mXySKkD)

Since Inspiration4, private citizens have continued to venture into space, participating in suborbital missions on Blue Origin's New Shepard and even visiting the International Space Station through all-commercial missions like Axiom Space's. While many private astronauts are financially well-off, efforts are ongoing in the space community to make spaceflight accessible to a broader range of people, including everything that MoonDAO is working on to make that possible to members of our community and the world beyond.
