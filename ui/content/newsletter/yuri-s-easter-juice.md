## Bite-Sized Space News

## JUICE is Gonna Fly

The European Space Agency (ESA) plans to launch the JUICE mission (Jupiter Icy Moons Explorer mission) to survey Jupiter’s moons on an Ariane 5 from French Guiana in South America. JUICE will travel to Jupiter to study three of its moons: Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/3WsG1WetN5cdndg6u8x7yK)

These moons are thought to have liquid water under their surface, and wherever there is water, there exists the potential for finding life. The spacecraft will study the moons for signs of organic molecules indicative of alien life, but the trip to Jupiter will take 8 years (arriving at the gas giant in July 2031), so we'll have to wait a bit for findings.

## Eyes on Boca Chica

After a few years of a fully stacked Starship orbital launch being perpetually just a few months away, it looks like we are \*actually\* on the cusp of this long awaited milestone. SpaceX has shared that their team is working towards a launch rehearsal this week followed by Starship’s first integrated flight test a week later, pending regulatory approval.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/rJf1acSdAeci385G1az7fT)

Elon Musk confirmed the same on Twitter and also said that it looks like the launch attempt is trending “towards near the end of third week of April.” Which would seemingly place the launch for somewhere around 4/20 which seems like an Elon joke, until you realize that according to the FAA’s Operations Plan Advisory, it might be slated for launch on April 17th, with backup dates for each subsequent day after through the 22nd.

A future with a fleet of fully and rapidly reusable Starships is a paradigm shift for space exploration and one that will be a huge unlock for all sorts of space missions, including permanent settlements on the Moon.

## An Important Anniversary

On Wednesday, April 12th, the world celebrates and recognizes the accomplishments of Yuri Gagarin as the first human being in space. Traveling in the Vostok 1 capsule, Gagarin completed one orbit of Earth back in 1961 -- 62 years ago!

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/b2q1u3M4HhbQtrEtAQXKFA)

Since then, humans have gone on to walk on the Moon, create multiple space stations orbiting Earth, sent probes outside the solar system, robotic rovers to other planets, and rocket launches have become a weekly occurrence.

**Space Trivia Answer: D) At least 90 moons.** Jupiter isn't just the largest and most massive planet in the solar system, but it also boasts the largest number of moons orbiting it.
