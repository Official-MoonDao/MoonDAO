## China's Shenzhou Crews

China’s Shenzhou 18 crew handed over the Tiangong space station to the newly arrived Shenzhou 19 astronauts, marking a smooth transition. Shenzhou 19 launched on October 29 and docked at Tiangong after a 6.5-hour journey, where Shenzhou 18 commander Ye Guangfu symbolically passed a key to Shenzhou 19 commander Cai Xuzhe on November 1. This new crew, which includes China’s youngest astronauts and its only female spaceflight engineer, Wang Haoze, will spend six months conducting 86 scientific experiments and spacewalks. Among the notable experiments is a test of a brick made from lunar soil simulant, which will arrive later in November to evaluate its durability in space.

China's Shenzhou 18 crew returned home safely on November 3 after more than six months aboard the Tiangong space station. Astronauts Ye Guangfu, Li Cong, and Li Guangsu landed at the Dongfeng site in northern China at 12:24 p.m. EST (1724 GMT), marking only the second nighttime landing at this location, which used infrared imaging for tracking. During their mission, the crew conducted various scientific experiments, including research on ancient microbes, set a national record with spacewalks, and installed shielding to protect Tiangong from debris. Their return followed a handover ceremony on November 1 with the Shenzhou 19 crew, ensuring the station remains continuously occupied.
