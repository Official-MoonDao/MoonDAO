## NASA VIPER

NASA has officially started building its first robotic lunar rover, the Volatiles Investigating Polar Exploration Rover (VIPER), at NASA’s Johnson Space Center in Houston. The team has started assembling the 1,000-pound rover, and engineers have affixed the rover’s lower chassis plate and the lower parts of the frame that will support all of VIPER.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/nEbfv7HTN9jUunUcgujtn3)

The Volatiles Investigating Polar Exploration Rover (VIPER) is a robot mission designed to scout for frozen water and other resources on the lunar surface. VIPER is planned for delivery to the lunar surface in late 2024 under NASA’s Commercial Lunar Payload Services program. The mission is scheduled to last 100 days or three full cycles of lunar day and night.

The VIPER mission will maximize its capabilities by taking advantage of commercially available hardware and open-source robotics software, including elements of its onboard science toolkit. VIPER represents the first resource mapping mission on another celestial body. VIPER is a mobile robot that will go to the South Pole of the Moon to get a close-up view of the location and concentration of water ice that could eventually be harvested to sustain human exploration on the Moon, Mars — and beyond. This is a critical mission in MoonDAO's Roadmap to the Stars.

## Accelerating SpaceX Launch Cadence

Last Friday, SpaceX launched two Falcon 9 rockets within the span of just a little more than four hours. At 12:26 pm local time, a Falcon 9 rocket carried 52 of SpaceX’s Starlink satellites into low-Earth orbit from a launch pad at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California. A mere 4 hours and 12 minutes later, another Falcon 9 rocket delivered two large communications satellites into geostationary transfer orbit for the Luxembourg-based satellite company SES from Cape Canaveral, Florida. This broke SpaceX’s record for the shortest duration between two launches.\
Just a decade ago, SpaceX was still a young upstart that had launched three times in a single year and was far behind competitors like ULA, Arianespace, or Roscosmos. Fast forward to today and those three organizations have *collectively* launched three times this year, whereas SpaceX launched three times in three days when you include the recent CRS-27 mission to the International Space Station. Today, the only other launch provider that can rival SpaceX with the number of launches is the government of China.

Space is traditionally seen as a very slow moving industry, but this is a pretty monumental shift in just 10 years – something that almost no one would have predicted in 2013.
