## Bite-Sized Space News

## Secretive X-37B

The SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket is set to launch the secretive X-37B space plane for the U.S. Space Force, following a one-day delay due to weather conditions. Liftoff is scheduled today for 8:14 p.m. ET from Launch Complex-39A at NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida.

This marks the seventh launch for the X-37B and its first ride on a Falcon Heavy, signifying potential implications for its upcoming orbital mission. The Falcon Heavy's increased mass-to-orbit capability and potential for a higher orbit suggest a new range for the X-37B's mission objectives, including operating in different orbital regimes.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/xbnxvv3edrDoYaXamf8Coq)

The X-37B, a clandestine space plane operated by the U.S. Space Force, has a history steeped in secrecy and technological prowess. Launched on various missions, this reusable spacecraft has completed six prior flights, primarily using United Launch Alliance Atlas V rockets.

Each mission has extended in duration, with the most recent, OTV-6, lasting a remarkable 908 days. With its upcoming seventh mission, named OTV-7, the X-37B will embark on its maiden voyage aboard a Falcon Heavy. Despite the clandestine nature of its objectives, it's known to house experimental payloads, including NASA's "Seeds-2" project, exploring the effects of extended spaceflight on plant seeds, highlighting the craft's multifaceted role in space exploration and experimentation.

This will be SpaceX's 94th launch of the year, putting an impressive 100 launches into the realm of possibility during 2023.
