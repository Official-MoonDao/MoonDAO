## Crew 9 Delays

NASA has delayed the launch of its Crew 9 mission to the International Space Station from August 18 to at least September 24, citing "operational flexibility" and ongoing evaluations of Boeing's Starliner spacecraft. The delay allows more time to finalize return plans for the Boeing Crew Flight Test currently docked at the station and to address potential issues with the spacecraft's thrusters and flight software, which may require updates for an autonomous return.

Additionally, the change deconflicts with a Soyuz launch and preparations at other launch complexes. NASA Administrator Bill Nelson expressed confidence in the decision-making process, as NASA continues to assess the readiness of Starliner and determine the best course of action for its crew. The final decision on the mission details, including the number of astronauts aboard Crew 9, has not been made yet.

## Axiom Space Crew-4

Axiom Space has announced the crew for its fourth commercial mission to the International Space Station (ISS), scheduled for October 2024. The Ax-4 mission will include former NASA astronaut Peggy Whitson as commander, alongside pilot Shubhanshu Shukla of India, Polish mission specialist Sławosz Uznański of the European Space Agency, and mission specialist Tibor Kapu of Hungary.

This marks the first time a Polish astronaut will fly to space in over 40 years. The mission aims to further international collaboration, scientific research, technology demonstration, and space commercialization, reflecting Axiom Space's goal to broaden global access to space. The crew will spend up to 14 days on the ISS conducting various research activities, launched aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket.

## VIPER Rover

The VIPER rover, a mission designed to locate water ice at the Moon's south pole, is currently in limbo despite being nearly ready for launch. NASA announced the cancellation of the mission due to budgetary concerns, despite ongoing commitments to fund the rover's transport to space. The decision has sparked a backlash, with commercial space companies proposing ways to save the project and a petition circulating in Congress with over 4,500 signatures urging its revival.

Intuitive Machines, among other private firms, has expressed interest in completing and launching VIPER. Meanwhile, Pittsburgh-based Astrobotic Technology, originally slated to deliver VIPER to the Moon, faces challenges due to this decision. Congressional intervention remains a possible outcome, with discussions ongoing to potentially reverse NASA's cancellation as the fiscal year end approaches.
