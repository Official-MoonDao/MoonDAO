## JAXA's Resurrection

Japan's moon lander, the Smart Lander for Investigating Moon (SLIM), has defied expectations by surviving its *second* lunar night. Despite landing at an angle that initially hindered its solar panels, SLIM has successfully awoken the vehicle once again and sent back new images.

![](https://embed.filekitcdn.com/e/5MgHd6kkhZ8fZBAgpuZgWA/ngwTkVrkgJF1DNBnuyFq8q)

JAXA, Japan's space agency, has nicknamed SLIM the "Moon Sniper" for its precision landing technology. Despite challenges and setbacks during the landing, SLIM's resilience marks a significant achievement for JAXA, boosting confidence after previous mission failures.

Surviving the lunar night poses significant challenges for landers due to the extreme cold temperatures, which impact the functionality of crucial components, such as batteries and electronics. Engineers must design landers capable of enduring these harsh environments, implementing thermal insulation and power-saving measures to ensure survival during the long, two-week lunar nights.

Additionally, positioning solar panels for optimal exposure to sunlight becomes crucial for generating power during the following lunar day. Overcoming these obstacles is essential for the success of all future lunar exploration missions.

## SpaceX ~~Triple~~ Double-Header

SpaceX had a busy Saturday evening, launching two rockets from Florida within a span of less than five hours. The first launch was a Falcon 9 rocket that deployed the Eutelsat 36D telecommunications satellite from NASA's Kennedy Space Center. Shortly after, another Falcon 9 lifted off, carrying 23 of SpaceX's Starlink broadband satellites from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station.

A third scheduled Starlink mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base in California was canceled due to weather, which would have made for a very impressive triple-header. Even so, the scheduled deployments really demonstrate the company's confidence and capability for rapid and efficient orbital missions.
