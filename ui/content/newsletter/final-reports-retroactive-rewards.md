After the project leaders made their presentations, each member of the senate made a recommended distribution to the other projects (those which they did not lead) as a share of the total retroactive rewards that they felt that project deserved (for example: 20% for Project A and 5% for Project B and so on).

Now it is up to the MoonDAO community to review the projects and vote on the recommended distribution that best aligns with their own vision on which MoonDAO projects are providing the most value. MoonDAO members have the responsibility to review all the projects that were completed over the quarter and distribute the retroactive rewards to the teams.

The final percentages per the Snapshot vote determine how much total DAI/ETH and vMooney voting power will go to each project (and thus each team member). This process will also determine which members get the new Core Contributor designation based on their impact in the DAO and continue in the Senate next quarter.

These retroactive rewards are a key part in how MoonDAO compensates contributors based on the impact of their work and your vote has a real impact. In this way we can reward results, rather than promises. ## Bite-Sized Space News

## Firefly Blue Ghost

Firefly Aerospace just announced it has completed the development and assembly of the Blue Ghost lunar lander. Next year, NASA plans to send ten instruments to the Moon via this lunar lander, marking the first mission in NASA's Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program.

These instruments will conduct various tasks such as sampling lunar regolith, testing GPS capabilities, assessing a radiation-tolerant computer's performance on the Moon, and studying lunar dust. The Blue Ghost lander is relatively compact, measuring 2 meters in height and 3.5 meters in diameter, with a payload capacity of 150 kg for lunar surface delivery and 2,700 kg for orbital missions. The lander's initial mission with NASA's science payloads is set for 2024, with plans for a second lander to reach the Moon's far side in 2026.

## Japan's SLIM lander

Japan's Smart Lander for Investigating Moon (SLIM) spacecraft recently completed a lunar flyby as part of its mission to attempt a lunar landing. During the flyby, SLIM passed about 5,000 kilometers from the moon's surface at a speed of 1.47 kilometers per second. It is currently on a looping orbit that will allow it to re-enter lunar orbit efficiently, with plans for a landing attempt in January.

SLIM's primary objective is to demonstrate a precise lunar soft landing with a lightweight design, and it carries observational data from Japan's SELENE orbiter for autonomous navigation during descent. If successful, SLIM will make Japan the fifth country to achieve a soft lunar landing after India's recent success with Chandrayaan-3.
