## Bite-Sized Space News

## Starbase FAA Lawsuit

The dust from SpaceX’s 4/20 launch is still settling. Today, numerous environmental groups filed a lawsuit against the FAA, saying that they failed to account for the damage caused by testing of the world’s largest and most powerful rocket, which results in "intense heat, noise, and light that adversely affects surrounding habitat areas and communities, which included designated critical habitat for federally protected species as well as National Wildlife Refuge and State Park lands."

The first test launch of the fully stacked Starship resulted in significant damage to Stage Zero, pulverizing the concrete pad beneath the orbital launch mount and sending debris, ash, and sand flying thousands of feet away.

The heart of the lawsuit hinges on whether the government should have required a more comprehensive Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) that could have taken years.

Over the weekend, Elon Musk did a detailed recap of the launch on Twitter Spaces, covering what went wrong, what went right, and what needs to be improved for future test flights. [Read the recap from Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/science/2023/05/elon-musk-provides-detailed-review-of-starships-first-launch-and-whats-next/).

## ispace Lander Lost

On April 25th, the space industry tuned into a live broadcast as ispace set out to make history by becoming the first private company to successfully land on the Moon. The lander had maintained communications throughout descent to the lunar surface, but then abruptly lost contact moments before touchdown.

Efforts were made to reestablish contact with the lander after a lengthy pause in the broadcast before the ispace team returned to acknowledge that the landing was likely unsuccessful. It is believed that the lander may have run out of fuel on descent and made a hard landing.

As of now, only the United States, Russia, and China have successfully put a robot on the lunar surface, all through government sponsored programs. But there are more lunar attempts on the horizon, including future attempts from ispace who completed a number of very important milestones on this mission and gathered important data for the next attempt.
