## Bite-Sized Space News

## Blue Origin Returns to Flight

Yesterday, Blue Origin’s New Shepard capsule successfully carried six passengers to the edge of space, marking the first human flight of the reusable suborbital vehicle since 2022. This milestone mission included 90-year-old retired Air Force Capt. Ed Dwight, who trained as an astronaut during the Apollo era but never had the opportunity to leave the planet.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GN9JQxdWoAAZAbz?format=jpg&name=medium)

NS-25 experienced a minor hiccup when one of the capsule’s three parachutes failed to fully deploy during the return to Earth. Despite this, Blue Origin assured that the vehicle is designed to land safely with just two parachutes, although a thorough internal investigation into the deployment issue is certain.

This recent flight happened after a lengthy pause in New Shepard’s operations due to a booster failure during an uncrewed mission in September 2022. The company's investigation identified an engine nozzle failure caused by excessive heat, leading to a redesign. A successful uncrewed test flight in December paved the way for this recent crewed mission.

A return to human flight by Blue Origin means that we are one step closer to the launch of MoonDAO’s second astronaut, [Dr. Eiman Jahangir](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFSsqP0H-ow), who was the winner of the Ticket to Space sweepstakes earlier this year.

## Wet Dress Rehearsal

SpaceX successfully completed a "wet dress rehearsal" at its Starbase site in South Texas today. This crucial prelaunch test involved filling both stages of the Starship with supercold liquid oxygen and liquid methane.

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/GOCvscJaMAEQ9-R?format=jpg&name=4096x4096)

"Launch rehearsal for Flight 4 complete," [SpaceX announced in a post on X](https://x.com/SpaceX/status/1792625975601054003), sharing photos of the procedure.

Starship's previous flights have shown incremental improvements. The debut flight ended shortly after launch when the stages failed to separate, leading to an intentional detonation. The second flight achieved stage separation and lasted twice as long, while the third flight lasted about 50 minutes before breaking apart upon reentry.

The main objective for the upcoming fourth flight is to successfully navigate through maximum reentry heating. SpaceX aims to launch this flight in about two weeks, although the timeline depends on obtaining a launch license modification from the U.S. Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and the conclusion of the FAA's investigation into the third flight's outcome.
