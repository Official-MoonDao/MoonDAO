## Bite-Sized Space News

- On December 17th, [South Korea successfully positioned the Korea Pathfinder Lunar Orbiter (KPLO)](https://www.space.com/south-korea-danuri-moon-probe-begins-orbit-entry) into lunar orbit. KPLO, which was launched by a SpaceX Falcon 9 rocket in August 2022, will study the Moon's surface and features starting in January 2023 using six scientific instruments, including a terrain imager, a Wide-Angle Polarimetric Camera, magnetometer, a gamma-ray spectrometer and a camera provided by NASA, called ShadowCam, which is able to see inside permanently shadowed regions on the Moon and will help plan future crewed and robotic missions there. The spacecraft will provide new insights into the Moon's surface composition, mineral makeup, and terrain evolution, as well as the nature and extent of protection offered by magnetic features from space radiation.
- [Nigeria and Rwanda have become the first African countries](https://www.state.gov/nigeria-and-rwanda-first-african-nations-sign-the-artemis-accords/) to sign the Artemis Accords, a set of principles for countries working together on the Moon. The Accords, which were developed by the United States and seven other countries during the previous administration, lay out guidelines for international participation for sustainable exploration of the Moon. The 10 core principles include peaceful purposes, transparency, interoperability, emergency assistance, registration of space objects, the release of scientific data, preserving outer space heritage, space resources, deconfliction of space activities, and orbital debris. The Accords are open to any country to sign and are not legally binding. They currently have 23 signatories.

## Gitcoin Passport

We have officially introduced [Gitcoin Passport](https://passport.gitcoin.co/#/dashboard), a tool for securely identifying users in online voting, as a requirement when [voting on our Snapshot proposals](https://vote.moondao.com/). However, some MoonDAO members have encountered issues, primarily related to not yet having their passports ready prior to votes going live. You must have your passport linked and ready to go with the requisite stamps, prior to any vote being posted.

These measures are necessary for ensuring [Sybil resistance](https://academy.binance.com/en/articles/sybil-attacks-explained) and the security of quadratic voting, as has been laid out by the [MoonDAO constitution](https://docs.moondao.com/constitution/). When using quadratic voting, we must strive to ensure that people are not creating multiple wallets to increase their voting power and manipulate the outcome. Although these measures may also create barriers for legitimate voters, so we must strike a careful balance in terms of what we require.

Our Gitcoin Passport requirements are kind of the bare minimum right now with social media accounts (Twitter, Discord, and Google – all tools we use frequently within MoonDAO) along with a record of an ETH transaction more than 30 days ago. The community will discuss adding additional stamps in the future to further deter bots and duplicate accounts.

[Set up your Gitcoin Passport here](https://passport.gitcoin.co/#/dashboard).

## Town Hall

[Listen to today’s town hall](https://twitter.com/OfficialMoonDAO/status/1604869097430274048) with some important governance updates and also an interesting conversation with Anastasia Stepanova, who shared insights about her multiple experiences serving as an analog astronaut on missions that simulate what it is like to travel or live in space.\
Anastasia participated in a mission called SIRIUS in Moscow, which involved living in isolation for four months while being monitored by cameras and subject to more than 80 experiments in order to study the effects of long-term space travel on psychology and physiology. She also participated in shorter missions to MDRS in the Utah desert and Flashline which is located on an uninhabited island in the Canadian Arctic, where she served as a health and safety officer and crew journalist. During this extremely remote mission, the crew had to be prepared for potential polar bear encounters and the possibility of difficult rescue or evacuation in the event of an emergency.

Anastasia is now studying at the Colorado School of Mines in a program focused on space resources, including the extraction of resources from the moon's regolith. During the town hall, we discussed some of the major challenges that need to be addressed before humans can sustainably live and work on other planets including medical technologies, food production, and stress management.
