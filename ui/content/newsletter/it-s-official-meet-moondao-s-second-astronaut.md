## Bite-Sized Space News

## 20th Flight

SpaceX achieved another groundbreaking milestone with the successful launch of the B1062 booster marking the 20th launch and landing of a first stage—a new record. Departing from Florida's Cape Canaveral Space Force Station on April 12, the launch coincided with the 43rd anniversary of NASA's inaugural space shuttle flight in 1981. The first stage returned to Earth for its 20th landing on the droneship A Shortfall of Gravitas.

Late last year, booster B1058 (with the NASA worm logo) was a reusable first-stage booster that broke a turnaround record and flew 19 times before tipping over in rough seas and high winds on its return to Port Canaveral in December 2023. B1058 was the first Falcon 9 booster to fly fourteen, fifteen, sixteen, seventeen, eighteen, and nineteen times. The booster had launched Crew Dragon Demo-2 (with Bob Behnken and Doug Hurley) to the ISS in 2020 and carried 23 Starlink satellites on its final flight on December 23, 2023.

## Delta Heavy

In a historic farewell, United Launch Alliance bid adieu to the Delta 4 Heavy rocket with the launch of a classified National Reconnaissance Office (NRO) spy satellite on April 9 from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station. The rocket, known for its three common core boosters, marked the end of an era after 16 missions since its debut in 2004. ULA, transitioning to its Vulcan Centaur rocket, described the moment as bittersweet, signaling the end of a legacy dating back to the space age's early days. The future of the launch pad remains uncertain, pending an environmental review by the U.S. Space Force.
