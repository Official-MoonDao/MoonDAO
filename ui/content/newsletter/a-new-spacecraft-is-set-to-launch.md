## Bite-Sized Space News

## Boeing Starliner

After years of delays (the first crewed test flight test was initially planned for 2017), today is the day for Boeing’s Crew Flight Test of Starliner, featuring NASA astronauts Butch Wilmore and Suni Williams. A successful crewed launch (there were two uncrewed flights in 2019 and 2022) would unlock a second American crewed transportation system to and from the International Space Station (ISS).

United Launch Alliance's Atlas V rocket will carry the Boeing Starliner spacecraft, with liftoff targeted for 10:34 p.m. EDT from Cape Canaveral Space Force Station in Florida. NASA will offer live coverage of prelaunch and launch activities for this mission, which aims to transport the astronauts to and from the ISS.

This flight test will allow NASA to evaluate the Starliner spacecraft and its subsystems during a week-long visit to the space station, a crucial step in certifying the transportation system for future rotational missions as part of the agency’s Commercial Crew Program.

A successful flight would see two commercial capsules in service, alongside SpaceX's Dragon which performed its first crewed flight test in May 2020. Having multiple service providers has long been the goal of NASA’s Commercial Crew Program.

## SpaceX EVA Suit

On Saturday, May 4th (May the Fourth be with you), SpaceX unveiled its long-awaited spacesuits designed for spacewalks, which will debut on the upcoming Polaris Dawn mission. These extravehicular activity (EVA) suits feature upgrades such as improved joint designs for mobility, enhanced thermal management, and upgraded helmets with built-in cameras and displays. [Check out the sleek video teaser of the new suits](https://twitter.com/OfficialMoonDAO/status/1786767783520333856).

The suits will be worn during a two-hour spacewalk, part of a private Crew Dragon mission led by billionaire Jared Isaacman. Extensive ground testing has been conducted, including placing the entire Dragon capsule with four “spacesuit simulators” inside a vacuum chamber. Modifications to the Crew Dragon capsule were also made to accommodate the spacewalk.

The Polaris Dawn mission, scheduled for early summer, will orbit at record altitudes, conducting experiments and testing communication capabilities through SpaceX’s Starlink constellation, as well as medical research

SpaceX held a live X/Twitter space with the Polaris Dawn crew to chat in more detail about the new suits, the testing and changes needed to Dragon for the planned spacewalk, and more updates on Polaris. Definitely give it a listen!

## Chang’e 6

Last week, China launched the Chang’e-6 mission aboard a Long March 5 rocket which aims to retrieve samples from the lunar far side, a first-ever endeavor. Initially planned as a backup for the Chang’e-5 mission, which successfully collected nearside samples in 2020, Chang’e-6 has been repurposed for this more ambitious mission.

The spacecraft includes international payloads and, unexpectedly, a rover attached to the lander. The rover is speculated to carry an infrared imaging spectrometer for mineral and compound analysis, potentially aiding in water detection. Similar to previous missions, little is known about how the rover will descend to the surface or its operational duration.

The mission timeline suggests surface operations are likely to commence in early June. Sampling operations, expected to last around 48 hours, will be followed by the ascent of samples to lunar orbit for rendezvous with the orbiter and eventual return to Earth. If successful, the samples could significantly advance our understanding of the Earth-moon system and the early solar system.
